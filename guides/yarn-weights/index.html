Yarn weight chart

Fingering, sport, DK, worsted, Aran, chunky: yarn weights have different names depending on where a pattern or a yarn comes from. The Craft Yarn Council's numbered system from 0 (Lace) to 7 (Jumbo) ties them together. This chart shows the names side by side, with typical yardage, gauge and needle and hook sizes for each weight.

Gauge, needle and hook ranges follow the Craft Yarn Council standard; gauge is for stockinette in knitting and single crochet in crochet (double crochet for lace). The yardage columns are typical values, not part of the standard: fiber changes them a lot. Cotton and silk are heavy, so a cotton DK has fewer yards per 100 g than a wool DK, while airy yarns like mohair or brushed alpaca have far more yardage than their thickness suggests. Lace yarn is usually knitted on larger needles than the table shows, for an open fabric. For needle sizes in other systems, see the needle and hook size chart.

DK vs. worsted vs. Aran

These three are the most common sweater yarns, and the most often confused:

  • DK (double knitting, weight 3) knits up at around 22 stitches per 4 in on 4 mm (US 6) needles. Light and versatile, good for sweaters, baby clothes and accessories.
  • Worsted (weight 4) is a step thicker, often around 18–20 stitches per 4 in on 4.5–5 mm (US 7–8) needles. The classic American all-rounder.
  • Aran (also weight 4) is usually a touch thicker than worsted, around 16–18 stitches per 4 in on 5 mm (US 8) needles, and named after the cabled sweaters of the Aran Islands.

Because the ranges overlap, two yarns with the same weight name can knit up differently. The label's gauge, meters per gram and recommended needle tell you more than the name, and your own swatch tells you the most. If you're replacing a yarn in a pattern, the guide to substituting yarn shows what to compare.

Find the weight of an unlabeled yarn

Lost the label? Two quick ways to estimate it:

  • Yardage per weight: weigh the skein, measure a length (say 10 m or 10 yd), weigh that too and scale up. Compare the result per 100 g with the table above.
  • Wraps per inch (WPI): wrap the yarn snugly but without stretching around a ruler for 1 inch (2.5 cm), strands side by side, and count. As a rough guide, about 14 or more wraps is fingering, 12 sport, 11 DK, 9 worsted and 7 bulky.

Then knit a gauge swatch to be sure. Knowing the yardage also tells you how much yarn you need for a project.

Frequently asked questions

What's the difference between DK and worsted?

Worsted is a little thicker. DK (Craft Yarn Council 3, Light) knits up at about 21–24 stitches per 4 in (10 cm) on 3.75–4.5 mm needles; worsted (4, Medium) at about 16–20 stitches on 4.5–5.5 mm needles. DK has roughly 220–275 yd per 100 g, worsted about 165–220 yd.

What is 4-ply yarn in US terms?

UK and Australian 4-ply is fingering weight (Craft Yarn Council 1, Super Fine), the typical sock yarn. The name once described four plies, but today it's a thickness, and a 4-ply yarn can be made of any number of plies.

Is Aran the same as worsted?

Both are weight 4 (Medium) in the Craft Yarn Council system, but Aran is usually a little thicker than US worsted. You can often swap one for the other, as long as your gauge swatch matches the pattern.

Can I hold two strands together for a thicker yarn?

Yes. As a rough rule, two strands of fingering come out near DK, two strands of DK near Aran, and two strands of worsted near chunky. Knit a swatch to check, since the result depends on the yarns.
