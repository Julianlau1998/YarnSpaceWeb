US or UK terms? Look it up

One of the most confusing things in crochet is that US and UK patterns use the same names for different stitches. A UK “double crochet” is a US “single crochet”, a UK “treble” is a US “double”, and so on, shifted by one. If a pattern comes out too tall or too short, the terms are often the reason. Check whether a pattern is written in US or UK terms before you start; YarnSpace has the US/UK crochet terms built in, so you can compare them without leaving the app.

Crochet hook sizes vary just as much: US hooks use a letter and a number (G-6 is 4 mm), while most of the world just uses millimeters. The reference in YarnSpace has knitting needle and crochet hook sizes, and our needle and hook size chart lists them all.

Tips for counting in crochet

  • Mark the first stitch of every round. In amigurumi and other spiral rounds there's no visible seam, so a stitch marker in the first stitch is the only reliable way to see where a round starts. Move it up each round, then tap the counter.
  • Know whether your turning chain counts. In single crochet it usually doesn't; in double crochet and taller stitches it often counts as the first stitch. The pattern should tell you, and getting it wrong adds or loses a stitch every row.
  • Count stitches at the end of a shaping row. Most patterns give the stitch count after each increase or decrease row. Checking it then catches a mistake while it's still one row back.
  • Swatch in pattern. Crochet gauge changes a lot with the stitch, so make your gauge swatch in the stitch the pattern uses.
